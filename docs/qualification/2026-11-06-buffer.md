# JJ buffer response (#101) — 6 November 2026

The supplied reverse-drive circuit produces a physical bipolar output. An
independent continuous RCSJ calculation reproduces its positive and negative
lobes. B02 undergoes a negative phase rotation; B01 makes an excursion and
returns close to its original phase. The output therefore carries almost no
net flux quantum. With the ports exchanged, B01 rotates and the output carries
almost one positive flux quantum per input pulse.

This investigation also exposed a separate startup-history defect, repaired in
4bf3f47e12d19e8d86fc6cd4f80ed114ed531eab. See the
[startup study](2026-11-06-junction-startup.md). That repair restores agreement
between simulation modes; it does not remove the physical reverse lobes.

## Circuit and independent equations

The [reverse input](https://github.com/JoeyDelp/JoSIM/blob/master/test/reference/buffer-101-reverse.cir) preserves the
active netlist in [issue #101](https://github.com/JoeyDelp/JoSIM/issues/101).
The [forward input](https://github.com/JoeyDelp/JoSIM/blob/master/test/reference/buffer-101-forward.cir) exchanges only
the source and output-resistor ports. Both use the active two triangular pulses:
0.82713 mV at 302.5 and 602.5 ps, each with 2.5 ps rise and fall times.
The issue's commented forward source has a different first-pulse width; it is
not used for this controlled comparison.

Define i1 from N1 to N2, i2 from N3 to N4, u1=V(N2), u2=V(N2)-V(N3),
and junction phases phi1/phi2. Kirchhoff's laws and the RCSJ equations give

\[
\begin{aligned}
L\dot i_1&=v_1-u_1,&L\dot i_2&=u_1-u_2-v_4,\\
C\dot u_1&=i_1-i_2-I_g-I_{c1}\sin\phi_1-u_1/R_0,\\
C\dot u_2&=i_2+I_g-I_{c2}\sin\phi_2-u_2/R_0,\\
\sigma\dot\phi_1&=u_1,&\sigma\dot\phi_2&=u_2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here L=0.125 pH, C=0.007 pF, R0=0.16 ohm, Ig=20 uA, Ic1=2 mA,
Ic2=1.5 mA, Rport=2 ohm, and sigma=h/(4 pi e), using exact SI h and e.
Reverse drive has v1=-Rport*i1 and v4=VIN; forward drive has v1=VIN and
v4=Rport*i2. Output voltage is v1 in reverse and v4 in forward.

The independent implementation uses ps/mA/mV/pH/pF internally and converts
its fixtures to SI, apart from the explicitly labelled time column in ps.
It does not call JoSIM, reuse its matrix stamps, or copy its integration scheme.
The original RTYPE=1 models have a lower transition boundary of 2.75 mV.
Reference maximum absolute junction voltages are 0.2784/0.3677 mV in reverse
and 0.8547/0.0291 mV in forward. Thus the assumed R0 branch is self-consistent
throughout this study; no normal-state approximation is being substituted.

## Reference qualification

`tools/qualify_buffer.py` integrates the six-state system with an analytic
Jacobian, splitting at every PWL breakpoint. It starts from zero at -10 ps,
uses a maximum step of 0.2 ps, and compares:

- Radau with rtol=1e-10 against Radau with rtol=1e-12;
- the tighter Radau result against independently implemented BDF at rtol=1e-11;
- componentwise atol=rtol/100 in the scaled units above.

The [SciPy solver documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.17.0/reference/generated/scipy.integrate.solve_ivp.html)
defines these methods and tolerances. At over 70,000 dense validation points,
the largest tolerance comparison is 8.25e-11 and method comparison 2.05e-10
in scaled state units. The acceptance limit is 1e-8, well below the JoSIM
comparison budgets. References contain 352 points per direction, concentrated
around both pulses with additional quiet points. Text hashes in the manifest
normalize CRLF to LF for native Windows checkouts.

JoSIM's finite negative-time startup duration varies with timestep. All
waveform comparisons start at 50 ps, after its bias transient has settled.
Startup correctness has its own independent equilibrium tests. This study
does not equate different finite warmup transients at t=0.

## Measured response

Each pulse uses a 60 ps integration window: 290–350 or 590–650 ps. Representative
first-pulse values from the independent reference are:

| Quantity | Reverse | Forward |
|---|---:|---:|
| Positive output peak | 277.772966 uV at 302.872 ps | 823.333088 uV at 303.878 ps |
| Negative output peak | -254.631646 uV at 306.241 ps | approximately zero |
| Net output flux / Phi0 | -1.3834923e-6 | 0.999997568179 |
| B01 phase change | -8.6927383e-6 rad | 6.28317002760 rad |
| B02 phase change | -6.28317371683 rad | approximately zero |

The second pulse gives the same response to the reported precision. Flux is
checked independently through both voltage integration and endpoint linkage:

\[
\int v_{out}\,dt=
\begin{cases}
\sigma\Delta\phi_1+L\Delta i_1,&\text{reverse},\\
\sigma\Delta(\phi_1-\phi_2)-L\Delta i_2,&\text{forward}.
\end{cases}
\]

The two reference calculations agree within 1e-9 Phi0. The specified input area
is 2.067825e-15 Wb, about 4.3 ppm below the
[exact SI flux quantum](https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/cuu/Value?flxquhs2e).
Finite windows and stimulus precision explain the small noninteger residuals;
the assertions do not require exact zero or exactly 2 pi.

The issue's first screenshot reads +276.817 uV at 302.96 ps; its second is
about +277.773 uV. The supplied netlist gives the latter peak for both pulses.
The original binary/settings are unknown. This establishes the reported
qualitative reverse response and its physical cause, not an exact recreation
of every screenshot sample.

## JoSIM checks and acceptance

The scientific owner checks 16 cases: both directions, both modes, and
0.02/0.01/0.005/0.0025 ps timesteps, against the reference at every output time
from 50 ps. The standard-library-only CTest group checks 12 cases at the first
three steps against the compact fixtures, plus fixture/generator provenance.

At 0.01 ps, maximum output-voltage errors are 19.1501 nV in reverse and
40.5556 nV in forward. Halving the step reduces voltage/current errors about
fourfold until output rounding matters. Reverse output errors at the four
steps are 72.7053/19.1501/4.88282/1.25925 nV; forward errors are
161.557/40.5556/10.1845/2.56514 nV. Both modes pass.

Bounds scale with (h/0.02 ps)^2. Their base values are 0.1% of input voltage,
0.1% of the larger critical current, and 0.01% of a phase quantum:
0.82713 uV, 2 uA, and 2 pi*1e-4 rad. Fixed allowances of 1 nV, 10 pA and
6e-6 rad cover the existing CSV precision and SI-versus-legacy constants.
The native check additionally requires voltage/current error after each
halving to be below 0.4 times the previous error plus a rounding allowance.

Other assertions cover finite output, increasing time, the prescribed input,
KCL, the subgap bound, pulse areas within 5e-6 Phi0, phase changes within
2e-5 rad in the full owner, and quiet loop flux within 1e-5 rad in CTest.
KCL uses 3 ppm of the largest branch current plus 1 pA for CSV rounding;
the measured full-owner maximum is about 1 nA.

GCC16 and Clang23 Debug/Release pass all 33 CTest tests. The new buffer group
passes ASan/UBSan with leak detection in 16.53 s; the unchanged production
source also passed the previous full 32-test sanitizer suite in 182.98 s.
All ten native binary/library jobs pass for descendant [7cbea62036df](https://github.com/JoeyDelp/JoSIM/commit/7cbea62036df6e0c7a1e76180c894cf26fb23be5) in [CI 42255843921](https://github.com/JoeyDelp/JoSIM/actions/runs/42255843921). These
bounded cases do not validate arbitrary junction models or noise spectra.
