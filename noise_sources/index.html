    Noise sources

    JoSIM provides Gaussian white noise and an approximation to power-law noise. Use .option seed=integer to repeat a run in a fresh simulator process. For worked circuits and measured setting choices, see floating flux lines and SQUID noise.

    White noise

    I1 out 0 NOISE(1p 0 1p)

    NOISE(ASD TD TSTEP) takes an amplitude spectral density in A/sqrt(Hz) for a current source, or V/sqrt(Hz) for a voltage source. TD defaults to zero. TSTEP sets the noise update interval, denoted by \(\Delta\). If omitted, it is derived from .NEB as 1/(3*NEB). The interval is limited to at least the internal simulation timestep \(h\). The default .NEB is 1 THz; .NEB must be finite and positive. An explicit TSTEP overrides .NEB for that source.

    Independent Gaussian values at the update points have variance ASD^2/(2*TSTEP). Values between updates are linearly interpolated. After the initial ramp, the time-averaged one-sided spectrum and total variance are

    \[ S(f)=ASD^2\operatorname{sinc}^4(f\Delta),\qquad \overline{\operatorname{var}(x)}=\frac{ASD^2}{3\Delta},\qquad \operatorname{sinc}(z)=\frac{\sin(\pi z)}{\pi z}. \]

    Thus the effective bandwidth is \(1/(3\Delta)\), with smooth rolloff rather than a rectangular cutoff. When the timestep floor applies, it becomes \(\min(NEB,1/(3h))\). Older versions used \(\Delta=1/(2NEB)\), giving only two-thirds of the requested time-averaged noise power. This correction changes seeded thermal and omitted-TSTEP white-noise waveforms. Explicit-TSTEP grid variance is preserved, up to rounding. Each junction thermal source is evaluated once per step; a source whose two terminals are ground now advances too, which can change later draws in the process-global stream.

    Samples exactly at the update points have variance ASD^2/(2*TSTEP); oversampling approaches the smaller time-averaged variance above. For \(K\) uniformly spaced samples per interval, starting at an update point, the expected sample variance is \(ASD^2(2/3+1/(3K^2))/(2\Delta)\). Setting \(h=\Delta\) therefore does not establish noise accuracy. Resolve circuit dynamics and the interpolated waveform, discard startup, and check timestep refinement. Output sampling and FIR filtering are separate settings: downsampling can alias power outside the output Nyquist band. Keep the seed, bandwidth and sampling choices in the measurement record.

    I(source) output uses the samples actually applied by the simulator. Printing does not re-evaluate a noise generator at past times. Source differences and output FIR filtering use the same captured history. Histories are retained only when requested, at about 8 bytes per internal sample per source, plus vector metadata. Output decimation does not reduce that retained internal data.

    Automatic thermal noise

    .TEMP T enables a parallel Norton current source for each resistor and junction. A component's TEMP= overrides the global temperature; TEMP=0 disables its classical noise. Temperatures must be finite and non-negative. Component NEB= overrides the global bandwidth. Both thermal source types use the white-noise interval convention above.

    The one-sided current ASD is \(\sqrt{4k_BTG}\). For a resistor, \(G=1/R\). For an RTYPE=1 junction, the active piecewise resistive slope selects

    \[ G=\begin{cases} 1/R_0,& |V_{pred}|<V_G-\Delta V/2,\\ I_c/(ICFCT\,\Delta V),& V_G-\Delta V/2\le |V_{pred}|<V_G+\Delta V/2,\\ 1/R_N,& |V_{pred}|\ge V_G+\Delta V/2. \end{cases} \]

    RTYPE=0 uses \(1/R_N\) throughout. Here \(V_{pred}\) is the existing explicit voltage predictor used to choose the step's linearization. The current sample uses that step's slope immediately, including on entry to and exit from the transition region. Positive temperature requires positive conductance; zero conductance contributes zero noise.

    The resistor relation follows classical equilibrium Johnson–Nyquist noise. Applying it to each junction slope is a local-equilibrium approximation. It does not model quasiparticle shot noise, quantum noise, heating or nonequilibrium gap physics. The transition-region choice is an explicit model assumption, not a microscopic prediction of switching rates. Classical noise also requires relevant frequencies well below \(k_BT/h_{Planck}\).

    The Norton source enters node KCL separately. I(R) and I(B) report the constitutive device branch current, excluding its internal noise source. For example, with an ideal voltage source and one noisy junction across the same terminals, -(I(Vdrive)+I(B)) recovers the injected thermal current. There is no separate named trace for an internal thermal source. See the bandwidth and thermal-noise study for measured checks and statistical bounds.

    Power-law noise

    I1 out 0 PNOISE(1p 1 0 1u 1 64)

    PNOISE(A1 ALPHA TD TSTEP TCORR N) approximates the one-sided power spectral density A1^2/f^ALPHA, with frequency expressed in Hz. A1 specifies the ideal interior power law's amplitude at 1 Hz, in the source's units per sqrt(Hz). The implementation sums independent Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) processes with logarithmically spaced relaxation times. Finite-band rolloff means the actual PSD at 1 Hz need not equal A1^2 when 1 Hz is near or outside the band.

    • ALPHA is between 0 and 2; 1 gives a 1/f approximation.
    • TD defaults to zero; output before the delay is zero.
    • TSTEP defaults to the simulation timestep and cannot be smaller than it.
    • TCORR defaults to the simulation stop time; zero selects that default.
    • N defaults to 48 and must be an integer from 4 to 512. Increase it when spanning many decades; the minimum count is not an accuracy guarantee.

    The nominal band is 1/TCORR to 1/(2*TSTEP). It must have positive width. Its edges have smooth rolloffs, with power outside the nominal band. The total variance therefore differs from the integral of an ideal power law cut off sharply at those edges.

    The OU weights follow the power-law integral, including endpoint approximations for the omitted fast/slow tails. Exponents 0 and 2 use the corresponding fast and slow single-pole limits. Exact sampled-OU gain is compensated before linear interpolation. This avoids normalizing to the wrong part of a rolling band edge. It corrects earlier large interior amplitude errors and changes seeded PNOISE/FNOISE/ICNOISE waveforms.

    Use frequencies comfortably inside the band and verify refinement for the measurement of interest. With the default 48 processes, the documented analytic scan across 2–8 decades gives less than 2.5% interior PSD error between 10/TCORR and 1/(20*TSTEP), for the tested exponents. A four-process bank can have about 50% interior error over eight decades. Narrow bands may have no well-separated interior at all. These are quantified approximation boundaries, not a rectangular-band filter or a universal error bound.

    Changing TSTEP changes the upper turnover frequency and total variance; changing TCORR changes the low-frequency plateau. Moving the entire band by a factor c, at fixed A1, scales variance by c^(1-ALPHA). In particular, 1/f variance is unchanged. Linear interpolation, simulation sampling and output aliasing still matter near the upper edge. See the colored-noise study for the equations, statistical acceptance criteria and controlled-seed measurements.

    Flux noise

    PFLUX loop 0 FNOISE(1u 1 0.25 0 1u 1 64)

    FNOISE(A1 ALPHA PHIDC TD TSTEP TCORR N) uses the same process bank. A1 is in flux quanta per sqrt(Hz); PHIDC is a constant flux bias in flux quanta. The returned phase is 2*pi*(PHIDC + noise) radians. The DC bias remains present before TD. Use this source with a circuit representation that relates phase to the applied loop flux; it does not introduce a separate magnetic-field solver.

    Critical-current fluctuations

    B1 out 0 junction ICNOISE=PNOISE(1u 1 0 1u 1 64)

    ICNOISE= supplies a dimensionless fractional fluctuation, delta(t), to the Josephson current. The effective critical current is Ic * gate_factor * (1 + delta(t)). Capacitance and shunt resistance are unchanged. Fluctuations below -0.99 are clipped, retaining at least 1% of the nominal critical current before gate suppression. Use small fluctuations when the Gaussian model is intended to represent perturbations about a measured Ic.

    The standalone function name ICNOISE(...) has the same arguments and waveform as PNOISE(...). The circuit attribute above also accepts that function name.
